# afdEP v2.1: Masked DeepEP adapted to AFD

Baseline: **Masked DeepEP** = the vendored DeepEP V2 kernels with `role_aware=False`
(union world, dummy Attention expert slots, upstream combine). v2.1 changes are
opt-in on the role path (`role_aware=True`, one NVLink domain); generic
instantiations keep the upstream template arguments and kernels.

## 1. FFN combine reduction

Upstream compiles the FFN reduction with a branch-free top-k prefix of
`ceil(top_k / ranks)`, where `ranks` counts the dummy Attention ranks, and unrolls
by four. In 2A2F that prefix is 2 of the ~4 slots each FFN actually reduces, and
unroll 4 spills (120–160-byte stack frames at the 64–72-register transport
budget). Role-path FFNs now use `ceil(top_k / ffn_ranks)` and unroll 2 (64-byte
frames, no extra spill). Attention ranks reduce nothing; they receive the same
arguments so every rank instantiates the same collective protocol.

Communication-only, 4 × H100, 2A2F, E128/top-k 8, capacity 2,048, BF16, graphs,
24 transport SMs. Median µs (Masked) and geometric-mean paired change of three
fresh-process repeats; parentheses count repeats faster than Masked.

| 2,048 random, screen B | 31.6, +0.1% (1) | 35.9, −2.6% (3) | 44.8, −3.4% (3) | 78.8, −4.9% (3) | 201.2, −3.8% (3) |

AFD basis: a role-path FFN reduces k/M slots per token (M FFN ranks, few by
design), not the k/R a symmetric R-rank world implies, and the union world's
dummy ranks are what set upstream's prefix to 2. Unroll 2 is part of the same
change: at the AFD prefix, unroll 4 spills (expected 4 with unroll 4 was 2–10%
slower than Masked at H4096). Unroll 2 alone would also help Masked; it is not
shipped on its own.

H2048 at one token is a tie. Before this change the role path (v1) measured
+1.7% to −0.8% against Masked in the same screens. Other screened settings were
not uniformly faster: expected top-k 8 was 3–8% slower than Masked; expected
6 was faster at 32–2,048 tokens but +3.1% at one token (H2048); unroll 4 with
expected 4 was 2–10% slower at H4096. They are retained in the record.

## 2. Entry barriers

Upstream dispatch and combine each open with an all-rank NVLink barrier plus a
grid sync, so every call waits for the slowest peer to arrive before writing. On
the role path both are implied by order (proof beside `afdep_skip_entry_barriers`
in `csrc/kernels/elastic/afdep_roles.hpp`): each rank runs complete
dispatch→combine pairs on one ordered stream, the main kernels launch without
PDL after their epilogues, and every exchange-(N+1) remote write follows an exit
barrier that each peer reaches only after its exchange-N inbox reads. All
role-path ranks skip both barriers; exit barriers, credits, counters and
lifetimes are unchanged. `AFDEP_KEEP_ENTRY_BARRIERS=1` restores them as a
measurement control.

## 3. Transport grid per bucket (rejected)

A full-device transport grid for buckets of at least 512 tokens was 10–16 points
faster than the 24/132 default, but the same knob speeds up Masked equally: it is
a generic DeepEP launch setting, not an AFD property, so v2.1 does not ship it.
The screens remain in [the record](validation-v2.1-grid.json). Sections 4–7 below
were measured while that policy was active: their paired changes compare arms
that shared it, but their absolute 512- and 2,048-token values include it.

## 4. Directional count exchange

Upstream dispatch reduces the union count vector (every rank and logical expert)
across all transport CTAs on every rank, sends it to every peer and waits for
every peer's vector, so Attention ranks wait for always-zero counts and FFN ranks
reduce their own zero counts. On the role path Attention sources reduce and send
only FFN rank/expert counts to FFN ranks and wait for none; FFN ranks skip their
own reduction and wait only for source counts. Prefix sums and host counts keep
their canonical positions (sources publish zeros). `AFDEP_UNION_NOTIFY=1` restores
the union exchange as a measurement control.

Paired change against the union exchange (sections 1–3 in both arms; three
repeats):

## 5. Few-token FFN combine

Upstream assigns one warp per received token. With unroll 2 a 2,048-wide BF16
row is four chunks, and each chunk waits for its slot loads before the next, so
a one-token reduction costs four dependent memory round trips while the other
transport warps idle (a globaltimer trace measured 1–5 µs per token on SM0/SM1 and
a grid-sync wait of up to 8 µs). When tokens are few, the role-path FFN combine
spreads each token's chunks over idle warps; each warp reduces and TMA-stores its
own slice, and the unchanged exit barrier flushes every slice. With one part per
token it runs the upstream loop. `AFDEP_NO_SPLIT_COMBINE=1` restores one warp per
token as a measurement control.

## 6. Directed exit

Upstream ends dispatch and combine with an all-rank barrier, so a sender also
waits for every receiver and every rank waits for unrelated peers. On the role
path, senders (Attention in dispatch, FFN in combine) flush their TMA stores,
grid-sync and release a per-peer monotonic arrival counter in each receiver's
workspace (the direct path's unused scale-out channel tails); receivers wait on
SM0 for every sender's counter to reach their own round. The section 2 ordering
argument carries over: exchange-(N+1) writes into FFN inboxes follow the
Attention rank's receipt of the FFN's combine-N arrival, which the FFN releases
after its copy epilogue and counter clears; writes into Attention inboxes follow
the FFN's receipt of the Attention rank's dispatch arrival, which follows its
reduce epilogue. `AFDEP_ALL_RANK_EXIT=1` restores the exit barriers as a
measurement control.

Paired change against the exit barriers (sections 1–5 in both arms):

| 2,048 random | −5.2% | −1.4% | −3.9% | −3.2% | −1.5% | −0.1% |
| 2,048 skew | −4.0% | −4.5% | −4.0% | −6.6% | −3.0% | −0.1% |
| 4,096 random | −5.2% | −3.3% | −5.2% | −0.4% | −0.3% | 0.0% |

## 8. Streaming FFN expand (rejected)

AFD basis: an FFN rank sends nothing in dispatch, so its copy epilogue could
expand slots while Attention sources are still pushing, instead of waiting for
the whole dispatch kernel. The candidate had sources release a per-slot ready
flag after each TMA store completed; the FFN dispatch kernel released its PDL
dependent at entry, and the epilogue waited for the prefix sums and then for
each slot. It was slower than its own control at every size (H2048, 2A2F):

| Variant | T=1 | 128 | 512 | 2,048 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Control (whole-dispatch dependency) | 19.6 µs | 30.3 | 67.9 | 185.7 |
| Per-slot release flags | 22.9 | 35.4 | 72.7 | 193.3 |
| Relaxed flags, no fence (unsafe timing bound) | 21.7 | 32.3 | 69.6 | 188.1 |
| Relaxed flags, back-off polling (unsafe) | 25.8 | 38.0 | 76.2 | 197.1 |

Removing the per-token fence recovers part of the loss but no overlap gain
appears, so the change is not shipped
([record](validation-v2.1-stream-rejected.json)).

## 9. Role-sized registered buffers

AFD basis: in one NVLink domain only Attention sources write FFN dispatch inboxes
and only FFN ranks write Attention combine inboxes. Union sizing reserves a
dispatch region for every rank (FFN ranks never send) and, in the rank layout, a
combine region for every rank (Attention ranks never reply). The role path sizes
dispatch for the N sources (source s writes region s - F; the copy epilogue reads
with the same offset) and combine for the F FFN senders. Every rank registers the
same maximum, as the symmetric window requires. Without the rank layout
(more ranks than top-k) combine keeps its top-k regions.

| Layout (capacity 2,048, H2048, top-k 8, BF16) | Registered | vs union |
|---|---:|---:|
| Union (Masked) | 34.6 MB | — |
| 2A2F | 17.3 MB | −50% |
| 3A:1F | 26.0 MB | −25% |
| 1A:3F | 25.6 MB | −26% |

Latency is unchanged against the `AFDEP_UNION_BUFFERS=1` control (H2048 random:
19.6 vs 19.6 µs at one token, 185.9 vs 185.9 µs at 2,048). The full 2A2F matrix
and both ratios pass ([record](validation-v2.1-buffers.json)).
